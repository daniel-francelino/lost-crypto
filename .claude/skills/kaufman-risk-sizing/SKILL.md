# Risk control and sizing (Kaufman, *Trading Systems and Methods*, ch. 23, 24, 22)

Page numbers are book pages (the PDF is the book + 20). **[Adaptation]** = ours for Binance USDT-M perpetuals, not from the book (futures and stocks). Chapter analyses: `docs/books/04-trading-systems-and-methods-kaufman/ch23-risk-control.md`, `ch24-*.md`, `ch22-*.md`; parts of those chapters (e.g. pp. 1062-1069, 1093-1098, 1103-1114, 1117-1146) were not read in the analyses and are not covered here. Complements `chan-kelly-sizing` and `chan2-leverage-risk`.

## 1. Luck vs skill, survival (pp. 1027-1032)
A windfall from a shock is luck; test how the account would survive the same move the other way. LTCM rationalized past shocks and failed at the next. The only way to reduce shock risk is **to be out of the market** and to **earn the most by investing the least** (a fully levered program earning 200% can lose everything after one shock; an unlevered one earning 20% has ten times the chance to survive).
Rules of thumb: risk a small part of capital per trade (**never more than 5%**, elsewhere 3%); know the exit in advance; big profits imply big risk; **do not meet margin calls**; liquidate the worst position first; stay consistent with your philosophy (a trend follower cannot take profit whenever it appears); plan contingencies.

## 2. Volatility target and required capital (pp. 1040-1050)
- Log daily P/L, compute its standard deviation, choose a **target volatility** (typically 15%; practical minimum 6-8%). Required equity = annualized P/L standard deviation / target. Expect losses of 2-2.5 sigma, so a 15% target implies a ~37% drawdown — aggressive; the liquidation level is often 50%, sometimes 10% (p. 1046-1050).
- Practical leverage depends on account size, not margin; broker margin changes slowly.
- Risk measures: annualized volatility; **VaR** (variance-covariance, historical, Monte Carlo; 1.65 sigma for 95%, 2.33 for 99%); correlation matters (two positions: US$17,068 vs the naive sum 11,650 + 12,475 at correlation -0.27). Return kurtosis can reach 25 => need **stress tests** (pp. 1040-1044).

## 3. Position sizing by volatility (pp. 1050-1054)
Equal money per position gives unequal risk. Three methods: (1) money / price; (2) money / (price x annualized volatility); (3) money / (ATR x sqrt(252)). Results differ hugely (BAC was 62x AAPL's size under method 1). ATR sizing cut BAC's equity volatility by 27% in a 200-day MA test. "Equal risk gives each trade the same chance to participate; if you cannot select the best trades, equal risk is the most conservative." Without size control portfolio volatility ranged 6% to 25%.
**[Adaptation]** Perps: size = risk budget / (stop distance in ATR x contract value), with `sqrt` of the bars-per-year for the chosen bar size (365 x 24 for hourly in a 24/7 market — the same principle as the annualization warning in `chan-backtest-hygiene`).

## 4. Stops and targets (pp. 1054-1061)
- Initial risk by system type: **trend systems accept many small losses**; breakouts trade larger risk per trade for higher reliability (**trade risk = max(High, n) - min(Low, n)**; shrinking it contradicts the method); mean reversion accepts large losses for high reliability, and stops reduce them.
- Stops sized by the system, timing, volatility or chart pattern — **not a fixed fraction of capital**. Options: parabolic, swing points, n-period high/low, KAMA, **3 x ATR(10)**. **Initial stop fixed at entry, not moved.** **Trailing**: fixed percentage (3%), volatility-based (ISAM suggests 12 x ATR(252)), **percent of profit** (50%, only after a trigger profit; use the nearer of that and the initial stop). Kase dev-stop (2-day true-range ATR + f x standard deviation, f = 1, 2.06-2.25, 3.20-3.50; closing basis).
- **Profit targets** are essential in short trades (noise), harder in trend following (long tail, requires re-entry); `E + f x V` from the **system entry point, not the filled price**. Euro futures 60-minute test: the slow 80-bar system was better **without** profit-taking (net 121,655 vs 94,405 at a 10x target, falling with tighter targets); the fast 5-bar system went from -3,278 to +19,098 at 7.5x and was negative at most other factors. Factors are often chosen by data mining. **Three-target partial exits** reduce risk when target distance is uncertain.
- **Kaufman on stops**: stops and targets are a duel with noise; use closing stops and intraday-peak targets; stops "work except when we need them most" (gaps, execution at the extreme); the likeliest to work **adapt to volatility**; **trailing stops are more practical than initial stops**; in random data, (number of triggers) x (distance) is constant; **proximity risk** (clusters of stops; big trend programs take up to 3 days to build); **management without stops**: reduce size as volatility rises (including switching costs); **risk overlay**: stops as a % of margin or account (1-2.5%) (pp. 1059-1061).

## 5. Measuring return vs risk (pp. 1034-1040, 52-59)
Criteria: higher profits; ordering of wins/losses; not penalizing big gains; shorter recovery; less time in market. **Sharpe/information ratio** satisfy only the first and hide sequence and jumps; monthly data look better than daily. Useful alternatives: **Calmar** (`ARORt / MaxDD`), Sortino (downside deviation vs minimum acceptable return), **Ulcer Index** `sqrt(sum D_i^2 / n)` with D the % below the prior equity high, Schwager's average maximum retracement, **drawdown probability DP** (daily standard deviation of drawdowns, only drawdown days) and the **drawdown ratio** `ARORt / DPt` (satisfies all three criteria). Risk increases with small samples, few historical pullbacks, few product groups and capitalization. Stability: the slope of annual returns (falling = deterioration). A 1% chance of a 50% monthly loss means a 50% chance within 50 months; with 100 cases there is a 10% error (pp. 1045-1046).

## 6. Ruin, capitalization, optimal f (pp. 1072-1092)
- **Probability of ruin** (equal wins/losses): `R = ((1 - A)/(1 + A))^c`, `A = P - (1 - P)`, `c` = capital units (60% hit rate: c = 1 -> 33%, c = 2 -> 11%). Unequal wins/losses (Gehm): `D = CA / E2`, `P = 0.5 + E / (2 sqrt(E2))`, `R = ((1 - P)/P)^D`. Needs many trades; history can understate risk.
- **Compounding with equity** leaves the account fully exposed at the top of the cycle (a +100%/-50% sequence earns nothing compounded). Alternative: keep exposure constant and let **reserves** accumulate; redistribute only once the drawdown has stabilized. **Trading the equity curve** (stop when the 10/60-day average of strategy equity falls) gave inconsistent results; first check autocorrelation of returns.
- **Optimal f**: `G(f) = P ln(1 + B f) + (1 - P) ln(1 - f)`; `f = [p (PLR + 1) - 1] / PLR` (p = 0.5, PLR = 2 -> 25%). Three losses cost 57.81%, not 75%, with fractions of current equity. Historical optimal f changes every trade; betting too much ruins, too little lowers geometric profit. Elder: never average down, never meet a margin call, liquidate the worst position first. Approximation `leverage = mu/sigma^2`. **"Consider these methods only to reduce leverage, not to increase it."** (pp. 1088-1092)
- **Expected vs actual**: with a 33% hit rate and 4:1 reward/risk, should you stop after 10 losses? Use the binomial `B(l; p, n)` and the run formula `(1/2)^(n+2)` to judge (pp. 1092-1093; the conclusions pp. 1093-1098 were not read).

## 7. Shocks and crisis rules (ch. 22, pp. 992-1000)
- Price shocks are the likeliest cause of catastrophic loss and the biggest gap between test and live. **In a shock correlations go to 1** (money, not fundamentals, moves markets); backtests treat shocks as normal days, and selection is the biggest beneficiary of shocks, which explains worse out-of-sample and live results.
- Case studies (Gorbachev 1991, UK election 1992, Gulf War 1990-91, 9/11): shocks do not always hurt trend followers; **prices often reverse after the shock is absorbed**; **volatility always rises**; a 16% jump in orange juice reversed 15% in two days (2012). Identify: a 1-day range >= 5x ATR.
- **Crisis management**: after an **unexpected gain** from a shock, **exit or reduce**; after a **large loss**, hold expecting reversal (can take days and raise risk); with enough reserves, add at the volume peak or when volatility falls. **System disconnection**: trend lines (50/100/200) turn down at the crash low and stay out of sync; do not hold a short dogmatically because the line points down — suspend trend rules, use predefined crisis rules and **reinitialize trends** when volatility falls.

## 8. Diversification and allocation (ch. 24, pp. 1099-1117)
- Diversification reduces **systematic** risk, not **market risk** (shocks): subprime proved it can vanish under stress. Perfect independence would cut risk to 1/N; in practice a fully diversified portfolio cuts initial risk only ~50%, and the benefit of adding assets drops quickly after about **4**.
- Choose unrelated assets; "financials" (rates, FX, indices) are highly interrelated; commodities react to dollar and rates. Strategies that look different (MA, ARIMA, point-and-figure) can all be trend followers — diversify by function (trend vs reversion).
- **Allocate on strategy returns, not prices**: mean-variance assumes a return every day; a strategy out of the market part of the time has zero-return days, lowering its standard deviation and earning a **larger allocation although risk when active is equal**; covariances of intermittent returns are distorted (two strategies active 50% of the time overlap 25%; two active 3% of days in the same market can carry high joint risk when both trade). Selective strategies (10-20% time in market) look weak but offer high return when active. The author proposes a genetic-algorithm allocator (GASP) instead of mean-variance.

## 9. [Adaptation] Bot risk policy sketch (for review)
1. Per-trade risk cap (<= the book's 3-5% as an absolute ceiling; with 1000 USDT use a much lower number), sized from stop distance in ATR; leverage is an output.
2. Daily P/L log per bot -> annualized volatility -> equity needed for the chosen target; start at the low end (6-8%) and treat 2-2.5 sigma losses as expected.
3. Stops adapt to volatility; trailing preferred; no early profit-taking on trend bots (see `kaufman-trend-systems`); breakout and reversion bots keep the risk profile of their method.
4. Shock rule: if a bar's range >= 5x ATR, flag the event; reduce after unexpected gains, hold per plan after losses, no new trend entries until volatility normalizes; liquidation price far beyond the stop.
5. Allocate across bots by return series including zero-days **and** by joint-risk when active; assume correlations near 1 in a crypto shock (BTC/alts).
6. Report Calmar, Ulcer Index and drawdown ratio next to Sharpe; use the Kelly/optimal-f figure only as an upper bound that reduces leverage.
